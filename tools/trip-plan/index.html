What is a hiking trip plan?

A hiking trip plan is a short written note you leave with someone you trust before you go. It says where you're going, your route, who's with you, when you expect to be back, and when they should call for help if they haven't heard from you.

When someone is reported overdue, the first things responders ask for are the destination and the expected return time. A plan means the person at home has both, so a search can start promptly and in the right place.

What should a trip plan include?

Include your name, your destination and trailhead, your planned route, who is with you, when you are leaving, when you will be back, the time to call for help, your vehicle and where it is parked, what you are wearing and carrying, and any medical information responders should know.

  • Who: your name, phone number, and everyone in your group.
  • Where: the destination, the trailhead, and your planned route. Add your backup plan if conditions turn you around.
  • When: the time you're leaving and the time you'll be back.
  • The deadline: the exact time your contact should call for help. This is the part most people skip.
  • Your vehicle: make, colour, plate, and where it's parked. An empty car at a trailhead is the first thing searchers look for.
  • What you look like: jacket and pack colours, plus the gear you're carrying, so responders know how long you can stay out.
  • Medical notes: anything a responder should know.

Who should I give my trip plan to?

Give it to one or two reliable people who aren't on the trip, who will notice if you don't check in, and who are willing to make the call. A partner, family member or close friend is usually best. A note left on your dashboard helps searchers, but it doesn't start a search.

When should my contact call for help?

Agree on a specific time before you leave. One to two hours after your planned return is common for a day hike; longer trips usually get a longer buffer. If that time passes and they can't reach you, they should call the local emergency number, or the sheriff or park dispatch for the area, and read them the plan.

Then do your part: tell them the moment you're out. A plan only works if "no news" reliably means something is wrong.

Is my trip plan stored or uploaded anywhere?

No. This tool runs entirely in your browser. Your plan is saved only on your own device so you can reuse it, and you can clear it at any time.
