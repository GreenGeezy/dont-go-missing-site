How do you calculate hiking time?

The standard method is Naismith's rule: allow 1 hour for every 5 kilometres (about 3 miles) of distance, plus 1 hour for every 600 metres (about 2,000 feet) of elevation gain. Then add time for breaks, rough terrain and a heavy pack.

The rule was devised by Scottish mountaineer William Naismith in 1892 and is still the starting point for most route-planning. This calculator applies it, then adjusts for your pace, the terrain and how often you stop.

How long does it take to hike 5 miles?

On a flat, well-made trail at an average pace, 5 miles takes about 1 hour 40 minutes of walking. With 1,500 feet of elevation gain it takes about 2 hours 25 minutes, and closer to 3 hours once you include breaks.

What is a turnaround time?

A turnaround time is a clock time you set before the hike at which you head back, whether or not you have reached your goal. It is worked out backwards from the time you must be off the trail, minus the time the return leg takes, minus a safety buffer.

Decide it at the trailhead, while you're rested and thinking clearly. Most people who get caught out after dark were "only twenty minutes from the top" when they should have turned around.

How accurate is Naismith's rule?

Naismith's rule is a reasonable estimate for a fit adult on a good trail in fair conditions. It does not account for breaks, steep descents, snow, heat, altitude, off-trail travel or a slower member of the group, so treat it as a minimum and add a margin.

  • Groups move at the pace of the slowest person. Plan for them, not for you.
  • Steep descents are slow. Coming down loose or rocky ground can take as long as going up.
  • Altitude and heat cost time. Above about 8,000 feet, or in high heat, expect to slow down noticeably.
